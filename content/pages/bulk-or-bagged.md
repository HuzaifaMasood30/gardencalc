# Bulk or Bagged: Which to Buy

Most garden and building materials are sold two ways: in bags from a store, or in bulk by
the cubic yard or the ton. Bags are convenient and easy to carry. Bulk is cheaper per unit
but arrives in one delivery you have to move yourself. This page explains where the line
usually falls.

## The trade-off in one line

Bags cost more per cubic foot but need no storage space and no wheelbarrow marathon. Bulk
costs less per cubic foot but you pay for delivery, and you need somewhere to tip it. The
right choice depends on the volume, not on the material.

## When bags win

- Small jobs: a few square feet of bed, a single raised bed, a patch of lawn.
- Awkward access: a courtyard or a back garden with no side gate.
- Staged work: filling one bed now and another next month.
- Small volumes of a specialist product, where a bulk order is not offered.

As a rough guide, bagged material makes sense up to about half a cubic yard, which is around
13 bags of 2 cubic feet.

## When bulk wins

- Large areas: a whole driveway, an acre of lawn, a run of raised beds.
- A single delivery point you can reach with a wheelbarrow.
- Work that has to be finished in one go, such as a concrete slab.

Above a cubic yard, bulk is almost always cheaper per unit, and the saving grows with the
volume. A cubic yard of mulch is roughly 13 bags, so any job needing more than two yards is
worth pricing both ways.

## Doing the sums

The comparison is not just the price on the shelf. Add the cost of delivery to the bulk
price, and subtract nothing from the bag price except your own time. Then compare the cost
per cubic foot.

| Volume | Roughly | Bulk usually wins above |
|---|---|---|
| Mulch or bark | 13 bags per cubic yard | 2 cubic yards |
| Topsoil | 18 bags per cubic yard | 1 cubic yard |
| Gravel | 1.4 tons per cubic yard | 1 cubic yard |
| Concrete | 45 bags of 80 lb per cubic yard | Half a cubic yard |

## Moving bulk material

A cubic yard of topsoil weighs around a ton, and a cubic yard of gravel around 1.4 tons.
Moving that by wheelbarrow is real work, so plan where the delivery will tip and how far you
have to move it. A tarpaulin under the pile saves a stained driveway.

If access is tight, ask the supplier whether they can deliver in a smaller load or whether
they will tip at the kerb. Some will, for a fee, and it is cheaper than discovering the lorry
cannot reach the spot after the material has been ordered.

## Storing what you do not use

Bulk material that is left in a heap will weather. Topsoil grows weeds, mulch goes mouldy at
the base, and gravel collects leaves. Cover the pile with a tarpaulin if you are not using it
straight away, and use the oldest material first.

Bagged material stores better, because the bag keeps it dry and out of the weather. A part
bag of mulch or compost will keep for months in a dry shed, which is another point in favour
of bags for staged work.
