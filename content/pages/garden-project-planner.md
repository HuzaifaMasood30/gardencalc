# Planning a Garden Project

A garden project goes wrong in the planning, not the planting. The soil is the wrong depth,
the path is the wrong width, the bed drains into the lawn. This guide walks through the
sequence we use: decide the shape, work out the materials, order, then build.

## 1. Decide the shape and the purpose

Start with what the space has to do. A vegetable bed, a play area, a path and a lawn all
have different depths and different materials, and getting the purpose clear first stops you
buying twice.

Draw the layout to scale on graph paper, or mark it out with string and pegs. A dry run on
the ground is the quickest way to find out that a path is too narrow or a bed is in the
wrong place.

## 2. Measure and work out the materials

Measure each area and run it through the calculator that matches the job. The
[measuring guide](BASE/how-to-measure/) covers how to break an awkward shape into rectangles
and how much waste to add.

Work out every material in one pass, before you order anything. That way you see the total
cost and the total volume, and you can decide what to cut if the budget is tight.

## 3. Order in the right quantities

Buy the whole job in one order where you can, so the material comes from a single batch.
This matters most for tiles and paint, where a second order can differ in colour.

For bulk material, check the delivery access before you order. A cubic yard of gravel is
about 1.4 tons, and a lorry that cannot reach the spot is an expensive surprise. The
[bulk or bagged guide](BASE/bulk-or-bagged/) covers when each option makes sense.

## 4. Prepare the ground

Most of the work in a garden project is in the preparation, and it is the part that is
tempting to skip. Clear the area, remove roots and stones, and level the surface. Compact
the base for a path or slab; loosen and improve it for a bed or lawn.

If the site holds water, deal with drainage before you lay anything. A path laid on wet
ground sinks, and a bed that floods will not grow much. It is far easier to fix before the
material goes down.

## 5. Build in the right order

Work from the ground up and from the outside in. For a bed, edge it, fill it, then mulch.
For a path, excavate, lay the base, compact, then add the surface. For a lawn, prepare the
soil, level it, then sow or turf.

Leave the finish until the heavy work is done. Mulch and gravel get walked into the ground
if they go down before the last of the construction, and fresh seed will not thank you for
being trampled.

## 6. Finish and maintain

Water new planting and new seed properly for the first few weeks, and keep off new surfaces
while they settle. Top up mulch and gravel each year, and refresh the compost in raised beds
as it breaks down.

Keep a note of what you used and how much. The next project is easier when you know that a
cubic yard of mulch covered the whole front border with a little to spare.
